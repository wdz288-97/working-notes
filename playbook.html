Analysis work starts in three ways

A KPI someone owns moves. Another team asks a question. Or a team sets out to do something, such as a loyalty programme.

All three run through the same loop of plan, do, check and act. This story follows the first, at a made-up chain of coffee shops.

Owning a number starts before it moves

A business intelligence analyst often owns some of the business's key metrics: tracking them week by week, explaining what moves them, and working with the teams who can act on what they show. Here, the analyst owns monthly orders.

The target is set with the business-unit head, from history, the business plan and the resources available, and the definition is aligned across every team, so everyone reads the same number.

Week by week, small slips add up

Then the shops run, and orders are tracked weekly against target and last year. Through September and October, orders slipped a little each week while last year's line held steady.

By the monthly review, orders are down 5.8k

October closes at 118.2k orders, against 124.0k a year earlier: down 4.7%, and below its 122.0k target. The owner of the number is expected to explain it.

A number is worth explaining only once it's confirmed

Before any explanation, the number itself gets checked: the same definition both years, complete data, and totals that match the master dashboard and last year's review.

A drop caused by a broken data load isn't a business problem.

Splitting the drop takes one query, inside one plan

With the total confirmed, the next question is where the drop came from. The AI writes the SQL inside a plan agreed first: tables, filters, grain and calculations. Its output is checked before anyone reads it.

Four ways of ordering fell, and two grew

The split follows how customers order: through delivery apps, takeaway, dine-in, app pre-orders, catering and the drive-through. Each row shows October 2026 against October 2025, and the change between them.

Delivery apps explain 83% of the drop

Ranked by the size of the change, delivery-app orders fell by 4.8k. This is the moment the story turns: one channel holds most of the answer, and a big channel is where action pays back.

Next comes a split by shop and by app, and a test of the operations team's explanation against related metrics.

The drive-through fell fastest, but by only 0.7k

Ranked by rate instead, drive-through orders fell 35%. That gets logged with its evidence and a cue to revisit if the drive-through becomes a priority, rather than leading the story.

Digging stops when more depth wouldn't change the action.

A finding holds only after it has been challenged

With the driver found, the loop turns to action, starting with the business-unit head. The head questions the finding with context the data doesn't have; each question is worth understanding before it's answered with data, until the finding holds or is dismissed.

The action belongs to the people who run it

Once the finding holds, a shared thread carries the evidence, priority and timeline to the head and the operations manager. The operations manager designs the action, here a delivery-app promotion, with data points supplied as it takes shape.

Agreement shapes each step, but it isn't proof of cause. The data keeps supporting it.

The review slide leads with the finding

By the business review, the finding and its action are ready to share. Documents are updated by the cutoff, the numbers pre-reviewed with the head, and who presents what is agreed.

On the slide, the title states the finding and the chart proves it.

Next month's number shows whether it worked

The promotion starts, and the weekly tracking picks it up, or doesn't. Either way, the next round of plan, do, check and act starts from there.

orders_by_channel_change.sql
-- Monthly orders by channel, October 2026 vs October 2025
-- Illustrative schema: one row per order
WITH monthly AS (
  SELECT
    order_channel,
    order_month,
    COUNT(DISTINCT order_id) AS orders
  FROM cafe.orders
  WHERE is_refunded = FALSE
    AND is_staff_order = FALSE
    AND order_month IN ('2025-10-01', '2026-10-01')
  GROUP BY order_channel, order_month
),
pivoted AS (
  SELECT
    order_channel,
    SUM(CASE WHEN order_month = '2025-10-01' THEN orders ELSE 0 END) AS last_year,
    SUM(CASE WHEN order_month = '2026-10-01' THEN orders ELSE 0 END) AS this_year
  FROM monthly
  GROUP BY order_channel
)
SELECT
  order_channel,
  last_year,
  this_year,
  this_year - last_year AS change,
  ROUND(100.0 * (this_year - last_year) / NULLIF(last_year, 0), 1) AS change_pct
FROM pivoted
ORDER BY change;

When another team asks

Requests come in through a ticket on a short template. Here is how one moves, from before it's written to after it closes, with notes beside the fields that matter most.

  1. 1
    Often the answer already exists

    Before any work starts, existing dashboards and past outputs come first. If one answers the question and the requester is happy with it, the work stops there.

  2. 2
    The ticket is filled in on the template

    The two notes mark the fields that shape the work.

    Data request template

    Requesting team
    Which team the request is for
    Objective
    What are you trying to understand?
    Description
    What makes you want to understand it?
    Next course of action
    What will you do with the output?

    What happens next decides how deep to dig. This field says what the requester will do with the answer. It sets the depth of the analysis, and ranks the request against other work by its likely impact.

    Example output
    Link to a mock-up sheet of the output you expect. Required.
    Criteria
    The filters, such as which items and which date period. Say whether you need it on a recurring basis.
    Dimensions
    The columns, starting with granularity: daily, weekly or monthly.
    Metrics
    The values you'd like to track.
    Special criteria
    Anything else to consider.
    Deadline
    3, 5, 7 or 10 working days, weekends excluded.

    The date is agreed, not just requested. It's confirmed in the next step and written back into the ticket.

  3. 3
    A written agreement saves the argument later

    Anything unclear gets settled in a quick chat, and the understanding and due date go into the ticket. If the date doesn't work, the requester takes it to the manager, so priorities are decided openly.

  4. 4
    A ticket closes once someone has acted

    The output goes out with notes on filters and definitions. A week later comes a follow-up: was it used, and what action was taken? Then the ticket closes. Expanded scope gets a new ticket linked to the first.
